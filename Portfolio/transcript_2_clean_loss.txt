Company (fictional): Harrow Analytics
Product: Predictive maintenance software for industrial equipment (vibration/thermal sensor data plus ML models predicting failure before it happens, targeted at plant operations teams)
Prospect: Plant Operations Manager at a mid-size manufacturing site
Outcome: Deal does not advance — no budget authority, timing is wrong, and a competitor is already mid-implementation

---

SR: Hi Marcus, thanks for hopping on. I saw you downloaded our predictive maintenance whitepaper last month, what prompted that?

Prospect: Yeah, we've been having some unplanned downtime on a couple of our older CNC lines, wanted to see what's out there. I'll be upfront though, I'm not sure how far this can go on my end.

SR: No problem, let's figure that out together. Tell me about the downtime first, how often, how costly?

Prospect: Maybe once a quarter something fails hard enough to stop the line for a day or two. Each time it's probably $30,000 to $50,000 in lost output, plus rush parts.

SR: That adds up. What have you tried so far to get ahead of it?

Prospect: Basic preventive maintenance schedules, nothing predictive. We inspect on a calendar, not based on actual equipment condition.

SR: Got it, so the failures are still catching you off guard even with a schedule in place.

Prospect: Right, because the schedule doesn't know what's actually happening inside the machine between inspections.

SR: That's exactly the gap we're built for. Can I ask, who else at the plant deals with this, and who ultimately decides on new software purchases here?

Prospect: That's actually the thing. I run day-to-day ops, but anything over about $15,000 has to go through our corporate procurement team, and honestly, capital software spend gets decided at the group level, not the plant level. I can champion something, but I don't sign off on it.

SR: Understood, that's useful to know early. Has corporate looked at anything in this space already?

Prospect: Yeah, actually, that's the other thing I should probably have led with. Corporate signed a group-wide deal with one of your competitors about four months ago, it's being rolled out plant by plant. Ours is scheduled for Q2 next year.

SR: Ah, so there's already a selected vendor moving through implementation.

Prospect: Right. I downloaded your whitepaper honestly more out of curiosity, wanted to see how our incoming vendor compares to what else exists. I probably should have said that up front, sorry.

SR: No need to apologize, I'd rather know now than three calls in. Out of curiosity, is there any flexibility in that rollout, or is it locked in at the corporate level?

Prospect: Locked in. I don't have the authority to run a parallel evaluation, and honestly even if I did, corporate wouldn't want to unwind a signed group contract for one plant.

SR: That's fair. I appreciate you being straight with me. Given that, I don't think it makes sense to keep moving forward on a demo right now, since there's no real path to a deal on your end before that rollout happens.

Prospect: Yeah, agreed. If the corporate vendor doesn't work out for some reason down the line, I'd probably reach back out, but I can't promise anything.

SR: Understood. I'll leave you with the whitepaper contact info in case that changes, and I won't add you to an active follow-up sequence given where things stand. Appreciate your honesty today, it saved us both time.

Prospect: Appreciate that, yeah.
